 Summary

I’m building Lexnify, a live SaaS product used by trainers and NGO event organizers to manage events and attendees.

Recently, I published a short visual post highlighting a production reality:
the application is slower than it should be under real user conditions.

The content wasn’t meant to showcase a solution or teach optimization techniques.
It was shared to document an important phase of product engineering — recognizing performance debt in production and taking ownership of it.

The carousel focused on:

the fact that this is a live SaaS with real users

the presence of measurable performance issues

a deliberate, planned approach to optimization rather than rushed fixes

The goal of posting this was simple:
to share process, not polish.

Performance issues are normal in evolving SaaS products. What matters is noticing them early, prioritizing correctly, and improving the system incrementally while users are actively using it. Sharing this publicly helps set the right expectations around how real software products are built and maintained.
